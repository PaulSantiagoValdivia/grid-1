# Changelog

What changed in Grid, for the people who use it. Agents have a changelog of their own, beside
the result contract in [docs/result-contract.md](docs/result-contract.md), which says what a
tool's answer holds and what may change in it. [RELEASING.md](RELEASING.md) says how a version
is cut.

A change people will notice writes its line, in plain words (what you can do now, or what works
differently), in its own file in [changes/](changes/README.md). The release step adds those
lines to "Unreleased", turns "Unreleased" into the release's section and records which versions
of the result record and the plan format it carries.

Every report Grid gives is labelled "General guidance, not your local rules". No release makes
Grid say that a plan complies with anything.

## Unreleased

- Doors and windows have kinds now (CNT-14): a door may be single, double, sliding, pocket, folding, roller or garage, and a window single, double, sliding, fixed or high-level, with the part of it that opens. Each is drawn with its usual symbol on the plan, the sheet and the DXF, and in 3D, the OBJ, the GLB and the IFC with its frame, its leaves hung from the face of the wall it opens toward and its glass. The door-swing check knows them: a sliding or roller door sweeps nothing. Say a door's handing as you would ("opens into the hall, hinged on the left as seen from the bedroom") in `grid_change_opening` or rooms text. `grid check` lists the doors with no swing stated as not checked, and asks about a wide door with no kind; the schedules and the IFC types carry the kind.

- A wall can be curved (CNT-18): give it an `arc`, its centre and the angle it turns through, and a round room, a bay or a stair tower is one wall with the right area, within a few hundredths of a percent. Doors and windows sit along the curve, straight across their hole; the drawing, the 3D view, the checks, the areas and the routes follow it. The DXF writes its faces as true arcs and the IFC its axis; where a file writes a curve as straight pieces, `grid export` and the file both say so. The room edits and rooms text keep to straight walls and say so when asked to change a curved one.
- Grid speaks Spanish: `--lang es` on `grid check`, `grid describe`, `grid draw` and `grid sheet`, `language` on the tools, or `"display": { "language": "es" }` in the plan, gives the report, the drawing and the sheet in Spanish with a decimal comma. A room's use may be written in Spanish (`dormitorio`); room names stay as written. Every note carries its figures as data (`values`). The Spanish is a draft translation, not reviewed yet (MSG-11).

- From a list of rooms and sizes to a first layout: with the rooms in the brief, `grid write --layout tidy` (or `layout=tidy` for `grid_write_rooms`) packs them in two rows along a hall, in a rectangle, writes the walls as rooms text and says each size it could not keep and by how much ("missed (must): "Bathroom" inside asked 1.7 x 2, drawn 1.70 x 3.10 m: 1.87 m² more."); `--layout sizes` keeps every size and lets the outline step. It places no doors or windows yet. A door written between two rooms that do not touch is refused with the line that would make them touch ("With line 3 written '… align east', they share a wall."). A wall height the rooms text does not state is no longer written into the plan file as Grid's default (BRF-6).

- A plan can say which rooms make each flat, in `flats`, and state each kind of flat once, in `flatTypes`. A flat may hold rooms on two floors (a duplex). `grid_areas` gives each flat's area from that list, never from the rooms' names, and the common parts apart: on the lab's four-flat floor, 68.27 m² and 99.23 m². `grid_schedule of=flats` lists the flats and the mix by type (how many of each, how many mirrored), and the room schedule says each room's flat. Each flat's outline is drawn on its plan, the check set ends with the flat schedule, and the IFC holds each flat as a zone of its spaces. In rooms text a unit can be a flat type (`unit one flat "One bedroom" "Flat {n}"`), so a flat type placed many times, mirrored, writes the flats too. `grid check` names a room in two flats, a room or a type a flat names that the plan does not have (CNT-4).

- A phone room scan becomes a plan: `grid write --scan room.json` (or `scan` for `grid_write_rooms`) reads Apple's room model, as an iPhone or iPad with a depth sensor saves a room it captured, and pairs each wall's two faces into one wall as thick as the gap between them. A wall seen from one side is taken as an outside wall of Grid's usual 0.2 m, grown away from the room, and marked assumed; doors and windows go on the walls the scan names; a toilet and a bath become fixtures. Every room keeps the scan's area (its floor outline, or what its wall faces enclose). The answer lists everything assumed, to confirm with the person, and the scan is kept beside the plan as `plan.scan.json`, keyed by its own ids, so a re-scan keeps the plan's ids for the parts it finds again (HND-12).

- Massing before walls: a floor can state its outline (`outline`), and with its level, height and kind of building it gives the floor's gross area, its slab and roof, its storey, the plot coverage and the distances to the boundary before any wall is drawn. With walls inside it, floor inside the outline that no room claims is said as such, and walls that reach past the outline are named. Floors can state how many homes and parking spaces they hold. The brief takes your own site limits (plot coverage, floor area ratio with the area measure it is set by, building height, parking per home), and `grid_measure what=site` reports each, with the plot's set-backs, as used, your limit, and met or not, naming how it was measured. Grid brings no limit of its own and suggests no layout; shadow pictures are not part of it (BRF-4)

- A wall can rise through the floors above its own: `through` names the highest floor it reaches, and it is one wall on each of them, so one edit moves it on all of them and the rooms of each floor list it. A window in it may pass from one floor to the next: the lab's stair window from 0.9 to 5.4 m is accepted as one window, drawn on both floors' plans, raised once in 3D, the OBJ, the glTF and the IFC, and open through the slab in a section. `grid check` says where a floor's outline overhangs or sits back from the floor below, and where a stair room or a lift shaft is moved from the one below, with the side, by how much and where; mark a floor or room `"overhang": "meant"` when that is on purpose. A window or door that cannot be drawn is now called a window or a door, not "doors or windows" (LVL-5).

- See the plan while the agent works, and point at it: Grid's preview shows the plan in 2D and 3D beside the chat in a host that shows MCP Apps, and `grid preview` serves the same page in your browser elsewhere. It draws again within 2 s of the plan changing. Click a room, wall, door or window, on the drawing or the model, and the agent can read what you selected; the agent can pick parts out and frame a room in your view. Every picture still comes back as an image with its text where the page is not shown (VEW-4)

- Ask how far it is from one room to another: `grid_measure what=route` now gives the rooms and doors passed in order and the length walked, from the middle of one room through the middle of each doorway to the middle of the other, or to the door out, round inside corners (Bedroom 1 to the Hall in the lab's flat: 4.34 m). `grid describe --length walk` (and `grid_describe length=walk`) walks the plan room by room from its way in: in each doorway, what is ahead, on your left, on your right and beside you, and how far; `--pictures <folder>` adds a picture at eye height from each doorway (VEW-9)

- A plan can hold a refurbishment: each wall, door, window and room may be `existing`, `demolished` or `new`. The drawing shows what is to be demolished dashed and what is new filled, with a key, in every look; areas, sizes and checks are of the building after the work and say so; a widened door is its old opening and a new one in the same place; `grid diff --since phase` lists what the work demolishes and builds; `grid_takeoff for=change` costs it as three lists, demolish, make good and new, from your own rates; the DXF puts each wall on its phase's layer and the IFC gives each element its phase. A fit-out can name the plan of the shell it stands in, `base`: the shell's walls and openings are drawn as existing and are read-only, so an edit that would touch them is refused (CNT-10).

- Work in your own units. Rooms text takes lengths as you say them, `14ft x 12'6"`, `360cm` or `3600mm`, as well as plain metres, and a `display ft-in` line (or `mm`, `cm`, `m`, with a decimal comma if you like) keeps your units with the plan: the drawings, the figures and now the PDF sheet are written in them. The agent is asked to take your units from what you say, and to ask only when it cannot tell. The plan file stays in metres (WRT-7).
- The README now says what Grid is for: a floor-plan toolkit built for AI agents, for anyone to use.
- A survey can be drawn from its own numbers (HND-11). A wall may be placed by a face (`"face": "left"` or `"right"`), with its thickness left out: a thickness guessed later grows away from the room, so guessing the outside walls 8 cm thicker no longer changes an inside size. A plan keeps what was taped on site in `taped`, a room's side along a wall or a diagonal between two corners, and `grid check`, `grid_check` and every change print each one taped against drawn, naming the ones that differ (`room/taped-differs`). A plan kept at survey coordinates states a point near the building as `site.origin`: the far-from-origin warning measures from it, the IFC is written from it, and the GLB holds the model near zero in one node moved by that offset, every point within 1 mm of the plan.
- A firm can keep its own standards, such as corridors at least 1.5 m, doors 850 mm clear or offices at least 9 m², in `firm-standards.json` beside its plans. `grid check` and `grid_check` measure the plan for each, as the use indicators and the room areas are measured, and list every part short of the firm's figure under its own heading, "The firm's own standards", apart from the code figures: the firm's own figures, not from any code and not tied to a place. The plugin for Claude Code and Codex carries four more skills as plain text a firm can copy and own: the firm's standards, a room programme, an option table and dimensioning a plan (COD-8).
- A plan can mark the rooms and routes that need to be accessible (`accessible`), and `grid_measure what=access` measures them as numbers: every door's hole and its clear width past the open leaf, each corridor at its narrowest, each room's turning space, and the way in from the entrance with every step on it and its height (a door's sill, a change of level, the ground when the site states it, the risers of a stair; a lift between floors where there is one). It says where it cannot tell and what it did not look at, and sets the numbers only against your own figures from the brief, each said as "your figure"; no code figure is used until a named person has confirmed it. `grid check` says the marked rooms and routes were not checked (CNT-17)

- Draw a section: `grid draw --section x1,y1:x2,y2` (and `grid_view` with `kind` `section`) cuts every floor along a line and looks to one side of it. What it cuts is solid under a heavy outline and joined into one shape, the ground line runs past the building, what lies beyond is lighter, and each floor's level and each room's height are marked. Slabs and roofs the plan does not state are hatched, and everything Grid took is listed under the drawing. `--marks levels,elevations,section` adds floor levels, elevation marks and the section's line to a floor plan (LVL-9).
- Write a room type once and place it many times: rooms text's `repeat` places a unit in a row from where its line says, with settings for the two end rooms, so the lab's twelve-room hotel floor is 17 lines. A floor line can copy another floor (`floor l4 "Fourth floor" level 4 copy l3`), and `through <floor>` stands a room such as a stair core on the floors above its own, walls and all. `grid edit mirror --turn 90` (or `turn` for `grid_mirror`) turns the plan a quarter or half round. `grid diff` says first when the plan is the earlier one mirrored or turned, and whether every room kept its size, instead of listing every wall as moved (WRT-4). A mirror swings the doors of an opening pattern with the rest, and `grid write` says when the walls it writes leave a pattern the plan keeps without its wall.

- A plan's site can hold the plot: its boundary, which edges are on a street, the set-backs you asked for (from every edge, the street edges or one edge, your own figures and never a code's), and what is on the ground: fences, gates, paths, paving, trees and pools (BRF-3). `grid check` says when the building stands nearer an edge than you asked ("The building is 1.00 m from the east boundary; you asked for 3.00 m") or reaches over it; `grid_measure what=site` gives the distance from each edge and each face of the building to the boundary and the plot coverage ("20%, the building's 90.00 m² on the 450.00 m² plot"). The plot and its things are drawn on the ground floor's plan in their own style, and add no wall, no area, no note and no storey height.
- The DXF opens in metres, in AutoCAD 2013's version, and "Baño" reads as "Baño" in every program, not as a code. A drafter can go on working in it: doors and windows are blocks that can be counted and swapped, walls are joined outlines with a fill, the sizes are real dimensions, every room has a tag with its number, name and area, and an A3 layout shows the plan at 1:100 with the text sized for it. An office names its own layers, colours, line weights, line types and scale once, in `dxf-layers.json` in the project folder (HND-1).
- `grid draw --facade north` (or `east`, `south`, `west`), and `grid_view` with `kind=facade`, draw one side of the building in elevation from the model: every floor at its level, the walls facing that side with their doors and windows, slab and roof edges, the ground line heaviest and faces set back drawn lighter, in the same looks and pen weights as the plan. A plan can state a facade's rhythm once, as an opening pattern in `patterns`: one opening with a count or a spacing and margins, repeated on the floors above if asked; it is read, checked, drawn and exported as the plain openings it places, and a pattern that does not fit says so (LVL-8).
- A 3D view can be asked for the way a person describes it: from a bearing or a side or corner, looking down so many degrees, from so far or with the eye so high, at a room, with a lens in millimetres, and moved with "a bit lower and more from the left" in one step. Every 3D picture comes with its camera, which says whether it stands in a room or outside any room. A view can be saved by name with the plan (`grid_save_view`) and called up again, the same, by an agent (`grid_view view=…`), by you (`grid draw --view …`) or in the app's 3D view; it is stored as Grid's setting and as the camera the open BCF format writes (VEW-3).
- "Which rooms get the sun" has an answer as a table: `grid_measure what=sun` gives each room's window walls, the way each faces (a bearing and a point of the compass, by the plan's north or, said first, the top of the drawing), the window area against the floor area, and how deep the room runs from each window wall. It is geometry, not a daylight result, and says so on every line (BRF-5).

- A floor can state its roof: flat, mono-pitch, gable or hip, with an eaves height and a pitch or a ridge height (LVL-6). Its walls are cut to the roof, so an attic's gables are gables in the 3D view, the OBJ, the glTF and the IFC, and a door or window whose top would stand above the roof is refused with the height that fits. `grid_areas` gives each attic room's floor with at least 1.5 m and at least 2.2 m of headroom, Grid's own bands; the IFC holds the volume under the roof, in its bodies and in the base quantities of its walls, spaces and roof slab; rooflights are windows in the roof. Dormers are not supported yet, and the plan says so.
- Keep what the person asked for beside the plan, in `plan.brief.json`: room areas and counts, inside sizes, the footprint, rooms that must or must not touch, and their own figures such as a door width, each with how much it matters (must, should, avoid), their words, and whether they said it or the agent assumed it. The agent writes it in one call, `grid_write_brief`, before the first room. After every change Grid says each line met or missed, as "asked 4.5, drawn 11.93 m²", never as a score; until the brief gives a room a size, a size in its name ("6 tatami", "12 m²") is checked against the room. The first plan written with no brief gets one line saying so (BRF-1).
- `grid diff` (and the tool `grid_diff`) says what a change did, by what stands where in the building rather than by ids: issues first, then room by room, then each change, with a picture in three colours. The same building under new ids reads as no change, with how many ids were renamed. It compares with a start marker set by `grid mark` (or `grid_mark_start`), kept in `plan.markers.json` beside the plan, so no git is needed, or with the last commit in the project's own `.git` (WRT-8).

- The plan says where it will be built, in `site.country` (`US`, `USA`, `United States`, `Spain`…), and `grid check` reads it on its own; `--country` (or `country` for `grid_check`) names another place for one check and takes the same names. With no place stated, room sizes and escape openings are no longer checked against the US figures: the report says so on its first line, with how many lines the US figures would have given. A place Grid does not know is refused, and the report names a place only by Grid's own name for it (COD-3).
- Walls, doors, windows, rooms and floors can carry facts of your own in `properties` (a fire rating, a department, a head count), and a room its number. They are kept through edits and `grid tidy`, listed in the schedules, and written into the IFC (a `Grid_Properties` set) and the DXF (layer `GRID-FACTS`). Three tools ask across them: `grid_fields`, `grid_find` and `grid_count`, and `grid_view` picks out or colours their answers (CNT-9).
- A plan can say where its values came from, given, measured, scanned or assumed, and how sure each is, in its `marks`. What was assumed is drawn dashed and listed under "To confirm" in `grid check`; areas from scanned walls say "from a scan, about ± x%"; `grid edit square` (`grid_square_scan`) squares a scan and says how far each wall moved. A wall that states no height is now described as "height not stated", and Grid no longer writes 2.7 m into a file that left it out (WRT-6).
- Fixtures are obstacles now (CNT-13). A door that swings into one names it ("Door "d1" hits "Table 2" (kitchen run "table-2") when it opens"), and `grid check` says when a fixture stands in front of a doorway or in front of a window above its sill. `grid_measure what=use` gives a room's use indicators: can a wheelchair turn, the clear width each doorway leaves, the way through, the floor in front of and beside each fixture and a blocked window; `what=route` with a `width` says whether a body or a trolley that wide gets through. Each says what it measured and against which width, yours or Grid's own indicator, never a rule.
- `grid check` and `grid_check` are short by default and about your plan: what to look at, the figures those notes used, and what each check looked at. `--full` (or `full` for the tool) gives the whole report. A note you have looked at can be set aside as seen, with why and who (`grid check --seen 3 --reason … --by …`, or `grid_mark_seen`): it stays listed under Seen, in `plan.seen.json` beside the plan, and comes back as new when its part of the plan changes (MSG-10).
- Lengths may be typed in feet and inches, millimetres or centimetres, or with a decimal comma, and are stored in metres. A plan's `display` (or `--units` and `--decimal`) shows areas, dimensions and schedules in the person's units: `12'-6"`, a sill of `48"`, `2860` (WRT-7).
- The IFC file arrives with its data: each room carries its number, its use and its net area with the rule it follows; walls, spaces, slabs, roofs, ceilings, doors and windows carry base quantities; walls are typed by kind and thickness with a material, doors and windows by size. Slabs and roofs Grid works out are marked assumed, and a plan at survey coordinates is written near the origin (HND-2).

Grid has not been released yet. The first release will hold:

- Every floor has a slab from its outline, open over its stairs, lift shafts and voids, and every
  part with nothing above has a flat roof, with a parapet when the floor states one: in the 3D
  view, the OBJ, glTF and IFC files, and `grid_takeoff`, whose slab and roof lines are marked
  assumed. A room can carry a ceiling with lowered zones, and the site a ground level: a door or
  window below it is named.
- `grid write` writes a plan from rooms text, with each room's size and neighbours, and works
  out the walls; `grid describe --as rooms` reads any plan back the same way.
- `grid check` gives one list of what to look at in a plan, with one count: parts that cannot
  be drawn, parts very likely wrong, what the checks found, and what they could not check.
- Every report, `grid_check` and the check sheet list what the checks never look at, ceiling
  height and which walls carry load among them.
- "General guidance, not your local rules" comes first in every report, list and tool answer,
  and is on every drawing and in every DXF, IFC, GLB and OBJ file. No line says a plan passes,
  complies or is approved, and a figure is given as the figure used here, with where it comes
  from and how far a person has checked it.
- `grid tidy` lists the walls that do not meet and makes the fixes you choose.
- Every note and pass that used a figure says which kind of building the figure was read for:
  today, homes.
- A plan can say what kind of building it is (a home, an office, a shop, a school…), and a floor
  can say otherwise for itself, such as a shop under flats. The home figures then run only where
  it is a home, or where the plan does not say, and the report says in plain words which checks
  did not apply to an office or a shop. Rooms have 27 uses, from office and classroom to plant
  and parking; a plain word such as "WC" or "staff room" is read as its use, and the person's
  own word stays the room's name. A room can say who it is for (private, shared, common or
  commercial) and carry the firm's own room type.
- `grid describe`, `grid draw`, `grid export` and `grid sheet` describe a plan, draw it, export
  it and lay it out as an A3 sheet in PDF.
- `grid init` makes a folder a Grid project, where the commands read its `plan.json`.
- An agent that meets a problem in Grid can draft an issue about it with `grid_report_problem`:
  the plan cut down and cleared of names and paths. Grid sends nothing; you decide whether to
  file it.
- Every pull request is checked on GitHub, on Linux and on Windows: the type check, the lint,
  the tests, the licence check and the scoreboard.
- Who sits where is kept in a file of its own beside the plan, `plan.occupancy.json`, linked to
  its rooms and desks. No export or sheet carries it; `grid occupancy` lists it by room.
- `grid serve`, or `grid mcp`, is the tool server an agent uses, with 30 tools.
- A room schedule (number, name, use, area, height, floor, with the totals of each floor and of
  all floors) and a door and window schedule that lists each opening once with the two rooms it
  joins, as text, as CSV for a spreadsheet, and as tables at the end of the check sheet.
- Quantities for a first estimate: floor finishes and wall faces by room use, ceilings,
  skirting, reveals, walls by kind and thickness, the outside face, doors and windows by size,
  and the slab, upper floors and roof worked out from the outlines and marked assumed, each line
  with its rule. A cost comes only from your own rates file beside the plan, which must name its
  currency; without one you get a rate card to fill in, and lines with no rate or a rate in the
  wrong unit are listed, never filled.
- Plans can hold several floors at their levels, steps inside a floor, rooms inside rooms and
  rooms with holes, such as a courtyard.
- A plan can say where north is, which way its y runs, which side is its front and where it
  sits. The drawing then shows a north arrow, the IFC file states that north (and no north for
  a plan that states none), and an agent can ask which rooms face east.
- The door-swing check stays quick on a floor of thousands of rooms, and the 2D view checks
  only the floor it shows.
- `grid check` says where an opening does not fit: a door or window that runs into a wall meeting
  its wall, a wall that ends inside a window, an opening as long as its wall, a door on a dividing
  line, and a window between two rooms (perhaps a borrowed light). `grid_split_room` will not end
  a new wall inside a door or window, and says the nearest size that is clear.
